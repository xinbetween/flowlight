Why Flowlight exists

Software on your Mac now decides for itself what to do next. Flowlight is how you see what it did.

On this page The storyPrinciplesWho makes itThanks

The story

For years, the question "what is my Mac talking to?" had a simple audience: people curious about an app that phoned home. Then AI agents arrived. They read your files, run shell commands and call tools, often while you're doing something else, and any of those tools can open a network connection.

In September 2026 a developer went looking for why a folder was eating 700 MB of disk and found that ZCode, an AI coding app, had been packaging entire workspaces, Git history included, and uploading them to cloud storage in the background. The company apologized, disabled the feature and open-sourced the app. But the discovery came from a disk-usage mystery, not from any tool built to notice.

The uploads were encrypted. The part that mattered wasn't: which app, which host, how many megabytes, and when. macOS exposes those facts for supported network flows. Flowlight puts them on screen, keeps the history, and alerts when an app, especially an agent, does something that doesn't look like its usual self.

Principles

  • Explain first, block only if asked. Flowlight watches and explains by default. It can also refuse — an allowlist, a rule about an app or a destination, a guardrail on an agent's tools — but only once you turn that on, and never for local traffic, DNS or Apple services. Every refusal is recorded, and every refusal carries its way out.
  • Metadata by default. Standard monitoring records hostnames, ports and byte counts while payloads remain encrypted. Optional HTTPS inspection is explicit, scoped and locally stored.
  • Local first. Your history is a file on your Mac. No account, no cloud, no telemetry.
  • Explainable alerts. Alerts include the app, relevant destination and evidence that triggered the rule. Flowlight does not use an opaque risk model.
  • Open source. A tool that watches your traffic should be one you can read. Flowlight is GPL-3.0.

Who makes it

Flowlight is an independent open-source project by xinbetween and its contributors. It isn't affiliated with Apple or with any AI provider named on this site. Ideas, bug reports and pull requests are welcome, especially new agents and providers to recognize.
