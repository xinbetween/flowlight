### What's new in 0.4.0

**Peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, named at last.**

Not all traffic goes to the internet. AirDrop, Handoff, AirPlay, Sidecar and Universal Control go straight to a device in the room over Apple Wireless Direct Link — no router, no internet, no address anyone else can reach. Flowlight was always counting those flows. It had no word for them, so an AirDrop of a 4 GB folder looked like a few megabytes to an address with no name.

- **Every flow carries a channel**: *Network*, *Peer-to-peer Wi-Fi*, *This Mac* (it never left the machine) or *Tunnel* (inside a VPN; where it went after that isn't visible here). Narrow a report to one of them from the Reports toolbar. The channel travels with CSV exports and with anything sent to a collector, where it is attached only to the rows that aren't ordinary network traffic.
- **Peer-to-peer rows are named by the feature that opened them** — *AirDrop and Handoff · nearby device*, *Continuity · nearby device* — because a link-local address tells you nothing and the device in the room has no name this Mac can learn. An app that uses the radio directly keeps its own name.
- **The two engines answer differently, and say so.** The sampler reads the interface each socket is bound to, which is exact; it also means the same socket on `awdl0` and on `llw0` stays two rows instead of quietly merging into one. The Network Extension is handed sockets rather than interfaces, so it judges by the address: link-local means peer-to-peer. That is the coarser answer of the two.

Nothing here is newly recorded and no new permission is involved — it is the same capture, finally labelled. Bluetooth (0.4.1) and USB (0.4.2) do widen what Flowlight watches, and will each ship behind their own switch.

### Install

Signed with Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

- `brew install --cask xinbetween/tap/flowlight`
- `Flowlight.dmg` — drag to Applications.
- `Flowlight-0.4.0.pkg` — installs to /Applications; offers to quit a running copy first.

macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon and Intel.
